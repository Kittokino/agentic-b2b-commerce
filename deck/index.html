Inside the rules, the agent acts. At the edge, it asks. Beyond, a person takes over. The red line is the rule, and the merchant draws it.

The three dots are the three lines of Nigel's order: cheese inside, salmon on the edge, the wine going beyond to Tom.

The problem

25–30%

of B2B transactions are digital. The rest still runs through people and messages.

Sales reps Service desks Email Phone EDI

Source: Sana case brief. Webshops alone have not changed this.

Orders arrive in every channel, and mostly unstructured.

Agents can take that work on, across chat, email, voice and the webshop. But in B2B an order spends someone else's money, so a good agent acts with judgment, and knows when to stop and hand over to a person.

Seamless doesn't mean the agent never pauses. Sometimes the pause is the design.

How might we

…let buyers order in the channels they already use, with an agent that acts on their behalf, and knows when to stop?

The only hard number is from the brief: 25–30% digital. I don't add statistics I can't source.

The twist to land: seamless doesn't mean the agent never stops. Trust comes from the right pauses.

Research

How we'd learn, before and after launch

Before launch

Tests 1–3 first

Measure & listen

Before launch tests the riskiest assumptions; after launch keeps the agent honest. The Wizard of Oz test is the cheapest way to learn about trust before building anything.
