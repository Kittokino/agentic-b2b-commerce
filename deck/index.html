What if an agent met buyers in the channels they already use, and knew when to hand over to a person?

Digital accounts for roughly 25–30% of B2B transactions. The rest runs through people and messages. WhatsApp and voice notes are our addition: that's how Nigel actually orders.

The cast · 3 of 5

The agent

Acts for FreshSupply

FreshSupply · within Ravi's rules
“Here's what I understood, here's what I'll do, and here's what I need from you.”

Does on its own

  • Understands the voice note
  • Builds the order from the product list and contract prices
  • Reads it back

Stops and asks when

  • The order is over the buyer's limit
  • A substitute changes what reaches the end customer
  • It isn't sure

Never

  • Approves spend over a limit
  • Substitutes without consent
  • Pretends to be a person

Channels

WhatsAppPortalEmail
Why it's in the castThe brief: an agent that acts with judgment, stops, explains and hands over.

The agent is an actor with limits, not a feature. Its quote is the pattern every message follows: understood · will do · need from you.

The platform

Two rules, set once, reused in any industry

FreshSupply · food & beverage
A medical supplier · hospitals
RULE 1 · STOP 1

Spend limit

Orders over the buyer's limit need the buyer's approver.

LimitHarbour orders over €1,500 need Priya's OK.
LimitWard orders over the agreed amount need procurement's OK.
RULE 2 · STOP 2

Substitution

A substitute that changes what reaches the end customer needs consent.

Consent fromThe chef or buyer, when it changes a dish on the menu or costs more than contract.
Consent fromA clinician, for any different product or device.

Same rules on the platform; each merchant's admin (Ravi, at FreshSupply) sets the limits, wording and approver.

This is the cheapest way to show "many businesses": one slide, two industries, same rules. The medical supplier stays nameless on purpose.
