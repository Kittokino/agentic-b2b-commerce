The agent listens, acts within the rules, stops at the limit and asks, and hands over to a person when it runs out of options.

Stop is the pivot, and the only red: knowing when to stop is what makes an agent trustworthy.

The problem

25–30%

of B2B transactions are digital.
The rest still runs through people and messages.

Source: Sana case brief

How might we let buyers order in the channels they already use, with an agent that acts on their behalf, and knows when to stop?

The only hard number is from the brief: 25–30% digital. The rest runs through sales reps, service desks, email, phone and EDI, and webshops alone haven't changed that.

Orders arrive in every channel, mostly unstructured. Agents can take that work on, but in B2B an order spends someone else's money, so a good agent acts with judgment and knows when to stop and hand over to a person.

Seamless doesn't mean the agent never pauses. Sometimes the pause is the design.

The places & the people

The Olive Tree

Kade Foodservice runs on Sana

The Olive Tree: a restaurant chain in Rotterdam with three outlets. Priya is the central buyer; Nigel is head chef at Harbour and orders by voice note after service.

Kade Foodservice ("kade" is Dutch for quay): a wholesaler running on Sana. The agent takes orders on every channel within the rules Ravi sets; Tom takes over when it runs out of options.

Sana is the platform under Kade, serving many wholesalers and manufacturers. The agent has a symbol, not a face: it never pretends to be a person.

Research

How we'd learn, before and after launch

Before launch tests assumptions 1–3

An evening in a kitchen 2 evenings
Watch how chefs order after service: channel, timing, words used.
Where Kade's orders come from 1 week
Analyse incoming orders by channel and time of day.
Interviews 6–8 people
Central buyers, chefs and service reps: who decides, and how spend is controlled.
Wizard of Oz on WhatsApp 2 weeks, 3 outlets
A person plays the agent. Do people accept, decline or work around it?

After launch measure and listen

Handled by the agent metric
Share of lines confirmed with no person involved.
Overrides and hand-overs metric
How often people change the agent's decision, and how fast hand-overs resolve.
Trust, in the moment in-chat pulse
One tap after a decision: “Was this right?”
Diary study 4 weeks
Three buyers record when the agent helped, and when it got in the way.

What I heardTo add: 2–3 conversations with a chef, a restaurant buyer and a supplier.

Before launch tests the riskiest assumptions; after launch keeps the agent honest. The Wizard of Oz test is the cheapest way to learn about trust before building anything.
